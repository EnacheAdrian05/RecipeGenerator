What is RecipeGenerator for?

Picture a scenario that's quite common, especially if you don't know how to cook or have just started learning to cook.

You're hungry, so you start searching the fridge, cabinets, and pantry for food.

Bad news: there's no ready-made food. You remember why - you finished it all earlier today.

Good news: you find a couple of potatoes, carrots, and onions, plus some leftover chicken breast. Lucky you - you could make a nice, warm meal.

Bad news again: you can't decide what to make. And if you're like me - frequently watching food content on YouTube - you may also struggle to settle on a culinary tradition (Desi, Thai, Chinese, Western, MENA, etc.).

You might consider buying a cookbook, but that often yields unsatisfactory results: it may not account for your specific allergies or dietary restrictions, and it offers limited variety.


This is the primary scenario RecipeGenerator was built for.

The app lets any user get an AI-generated recipe, using the [TO BE ADDED] model, based on the ingredients they have and the cultural region they'd like inspiration from.

And - because LLM models are non-deterministic - you can get multiple recipes from the same ingredients and region, giving you healthy variety.

Better still, by creating an account you can list your allergies and the dietary restrictions you follow once. That way you don't have to re-enter them for every recipe.

What makes RecipeGenerator different?

There are other AI recipe generators, such as FoodsGPT and dishgen .

The biggest issue I find with them is that they don't store user data about allergies and dietary restrictions, so the AI model has no baseline context across prompts.

That means the user has to supply the full context for every single recipe, which is inconvenient.

They also fail to account for religion in the context of dietary restrictions - a gap that, depending on the user's culture, can have serious consequences.
